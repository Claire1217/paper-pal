\begin{abstract}
Urban songbirds are widely reported to sing earlier than their rural counterparts, but it remains unclear whether artificial light or traffic noise drives the shift. We recorded the dawn chorus of common blackbirds at 48 sites along a lighting gradient over three breeding seasons. Song onset advanced by 4.1 minutes for each tenfold increase in night-time illuminance, and the effect held after controlling for noise, temperature, and date. Birds at the brightest sites began singing almost half an hour before those in unlit parks. These results suggest that light, not noise, sets the urban dawn, and that modest reductions in street lighting could restore a substantial part of the natural schedule.
\end{abstract}
